# abstract:
Foraging for resources is a universal animal behavior that utilizes important cognitive capabilities involved in more complex goal directed behavior, making it a excellent behavioral paradigm for understanding the building blocks of these more complex cognitive functions. In a patchy foraging environment, animals must make decisions about whether to exploit current resource patches, or explore in hopes of finding a higher yielding resource patch. Many normative models detailing the optimal behavior of agents in these environments have been developed, but these models are unable to capture the observed behavior we see in animals. In order to more accurately describe observed animal behavior, we develop various models of a patch foraging task using both MDP and POMDP frameworks, which incorporate various levels of uncertainty in the decision making process. We find that introducing uncertainty results in certain animal behaviors becoming captured by the model, while missing out on other behaviors.
